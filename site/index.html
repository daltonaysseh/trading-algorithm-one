What did management's tone actually say?

Search a ticker to read its most recent earnings call scored across 5 communication dimensions — how clearly they guided, how much they hedged, how directly they answered questions, whether Q&A matched the prepared script, and how they disclosed risk.

Not analyzed yet? Ask Claude in your session — e.g. "analyze PBF" — any ticker with a public earnings call can be added.

This is not a trading signal. It doesn't predict stock price movement, it isn't investment advice, and it hasn't been backtested against returns — it's a description of how a call was communicated, not what the stock will do next. See Limitations for what this rubric hasn't been checked against.

How it works

  1. Search any ticker (or ask Claude directly, e.g. "analyze PBF"). The tool finds that company's most recent earnings call.
  2. The transcript is split into prepared remarks and live Q&A.
  3. Each section is read and scored on 5 dimensions (see Rubric), with a specific excerpt kept as evidence for the most notable score.
  4. Prepared-remarks tone is explicitly compared to Q&A tone — a confident script paired with an evasive Q&A is exactly the kind of divergence this is built to surface.
  5. Results are cached, so re-analyzing the same call is instant, and a history builds up per ticker as more of its calls get analyzed.

For a deeper explanation of what each dimension measures, why it matters, and real examples from analyzed calls, see How It Works.
